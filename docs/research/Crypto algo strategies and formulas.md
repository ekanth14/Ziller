# Few missing crypto edges survive honest costs

The useful additions to this terminal are mostly **risk tools, exits and statistics, not new alphas**. Of the publicly documented strategy families the terminal lacks, only **cross-sectional (CS) momentum in large coins** has peer-reviewed, multi-coin crypto evidence. Even there, published long-short alphas come largely from short legs and shrink or vanish net of costs. **Turn-of-month** has peer-reviewed gross evidence that ends in 2021. Dual momentum, 10-day-high continuation and intraday seasonality rest on in-sample, cost-free practitioner backtests. ORB, NR7, inside bar, Connors RSI, %b, z-score, OBV/CMF and taker-flow rules are precisely defined but have **no credible crypto test at all**. The stat-arb results that survive costs and out-of-sample testing (Tadi & Witzany's copula pairs at Sharpe 0.97, Tadi & Kortchemski's cointegration and Johansen baskets) all ran on futures with a short leg, so a long-only spot terminal cannot replicate them. The items with the most solid support are **volatility targeting** (60+ assets, decades of data), **Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio and Minimum Track Record Length** (they correct Sharpe for crypto's fat tails), and Kaminski & Lo's theorem that stops add value only under momentum. Those three, plus a BTC regime gate and wide volatility-scaled trailing exits, are where to start. The gate (≥100 out-of-sample trades, Deflated Sharpe p<0.05, PBO<0.3, positive expectancy after 0.1% fees per side) rejects several popular ideas on arithmetic alone, before any backtest runs.

Cross-sectional momentum leads the new-strategy list on breadth of evidence. Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu test 25 equity-style factors and find that **market, size and momentum explain the crypto cross-section, with momentum stronger in larger coins** ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882.pdf)). Dobrynskaya, using the 2,000 largest coins over 2014–2020, finds **momentum out to 2–4 weeks and significant reversal beyond one month** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3913263)). The decisive caveat for this terminal comes from a 3,956-coin study: crypto momentum **incurs substantial trading costs and extracts alphas largely from short positions** ([IRFA 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924001509)). The JFQA trend factor (CTREND) combines price and volume technicals into a stronger cross-sectional model ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)). A 2026 replication, readable only from its search snippet, reports a **net long-short weekly return of −0.87% at the lowest assumed costs** ([arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01951)). The literature therefore supports momentum as a *ranking* signal among liquid coins, and does not support it as a cost-proof alpha. The most relevant test, a net-of-costs replication on Binance-tradable coins for 2020–2026, could not be read ([SSRN 7404139](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7404139)). The sources also contradict each other: summaries of Grobys & Sapkota (2019) describe both negative, insignificant momentum and roughly 3%/week payoffs ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v180y2019icp6-10.html)), so read the original before citing it either way.

One pattern runs through this table: **every documented drawdown reduction comes from staying out of the market, not from picking entries better**. Dual momentum's absolute leg, the 10-day-high condition and the volatility cap each cut maximum drawdown by 30–40 percentage points in-sample ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dual-momentum-allocation-between-physical-gold-and-bitcoin-digital-gold/)). The vol-capped dual momentum variant shows the cost of that protection: **MDD fell from −49% to −12%, and CAGR fell from 65% to 12%**. Long-only crypto P&L is a beta trade with a timing switch. Gates and sizing are what to engineer, and new entry triggers come second.

## Stat-arb loses its hedge on spot, but the ML plumbing transfers

Stat-arb has the best-documented results of anything the terminal is missing, and the worst fit for a long-only spot book. Tadi & Witzany's copula method ran on 20 Binance USDT-M futures with hourly bars, 3-week formation and 1-week trading windows over 104 rolling cycles. At α₁ = 0.10 it produced **37.1% annualized, Sharpe 0.97, max drawdown −35.6%, 176 trades, with costs at 11.7% of gross P&L**. Looser thresholds collapsed to Sharpe 0.16, with costs eating 47.7% of gross P&L ([arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961)). Tadi & Kortchemski report pairs Sharpe of 6.96 and a Johansen basket where **0.81 of 1.44 XBT of realized P&L went to commission** ([arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662)). Their sample is 39 weeks on one exchange, and Sharpe ratios that high should fail a Deflated Sharpe test with honest trial counts. At daily frequency the distance method earns **−0.07% per month** on 26 Binance coins; profit appears only at 5-minute frequency and is "quite sensitive" to costs ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c)). Kalman hedging raises turnover about **4.5×** over rolling least squares. In Palomar's equity example the ranking **reverses at 10 bps costs**: 0.33 vs 0.41 cumulative return ([Palomar](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html)). Spot crypto costs 10 bps per side, so this case applies directly.

The López de Prado pipeline is fully specified. The terminal already has triple-barrier labels, CPCV, PBO and DSR, so the missing pieces are small. **Fractional differentiation** uses weights `ω₀ = 1, ω_k = −ω_{k−1}(d−k+1)/k`, cut off at |ω| < 1e-5 (fixed window). Choose d as the minimum value on a 0–1 grid (step 0.05) whose ADF statistic falls below the 95% critical value, and re-estimate it inside each walk-forward fold ([mlfinpy](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html); [arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2)). **Meta-labeling** is natively long-only: the existing rule sets side = +1, triple-barrier assigns 1/0, and a secondary RF or GBM predicts P(win) to filter trades ([Hudson & Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/?p=3454)). The remaining formulas, flagged **[unverified this session, AFML recall]**, are CUSUM event sampling `S⁺_t = max(0, S⁺_{t−1} + y_t − E[y_t])` with threshold h ≈ 1–2× daily vol, and bet sizing `z = (p−½)/√(p(1−p))`, `m = 2Φ(z) − 1`, clipped to [0,1] and discretized in 0.05–0.1 steps. Uniqueness weights are `ū_i = mean(1/c_t)` over each label's life, used as `max_samples` in the RF. Purged k-fold drops overlapping label intervals and adds an embargo of about 0.01·T bars. The crypto evidence is thin and flattered by bull markets. Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski report BTC 30-minute IR 2.03 net of 5 bps costs, but say themselves that **the test set "remained a highly bullish time"** and that slippage was ignored ([arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2)). A 2026 RF/XGBoost/LightGBM preprint shows **gross Sharpe 0.14 on 185 trades**, which fees would turn negative ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/384a120c45428509aa846cf34b2c8121/download_pub)). Meta-labeling also cannot create an edge where the primary model has none ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14706/why-meta-labeling-is-not-a-silver-bullet/)).

Regime switching gets a concrete upgrade. **Statistical jump models** add a penalty λ per state change, minimizing `Σ‖x_t − θ_{s_t}‖² + λ·Σ1{s_t ≠ s_{t−1}}`. The objective is reproduced from recall and is **unverified**; λ is chosen by walk-forward CV on net strategy Sharpe. In an out-of-sample test on US, German and Japanese equities from 1990 to 2023, with costs and trading delays, jump models beat both HMM-guided allocation and buy-and-hold on volatility, drawdown and Sharpe ([arXiv 2402.05272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05272)). This matters for the existing HMM because state flicker generates fee-eating turnover. A practitioner Markov-switching strategy on BTC/ETH 4h bars **lost money out of sample** ([audit](https://hallengray.github.io/signal-independence-audit/MSS_POST.html)). Drive signals with filtered probabilities only, never smoothed ones, trade with a one-bar delay, and use the regime as a long/flat switch.

## Exits and sizing: wide volatility-scaled exits and vol targets have theory behind them

Kaminski & Lo settle the conceptual question. Under a random walk **every 0/1 stop-loss rule strictly reduces expected return**. Stops can add return only when returns have momentum or regime switching, and empirically they help at longer frequencies and wider thresholds ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp234-254.html)). Crypto time-series momentum is strong but **concentrated in bull markets** ([AUT/ACFR](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). That points to wide, ratcheting volatility stops on 4h–1d trend systems and argues against tight fixed-percentage stops on 15m bars. No peer-reviewed crypto study isolates trailing-stop value. One practitioner bot found trailing stops did about 8% worse than a fixed −3.5% stop because of whipsaw ([DEV](https://dev.to/trendrider/i-tested-4-stop-loss-methods-on-my-crypto-bot-heres-what-actually-works-47i0)). The AdaptiveTrend preprint's **Sharpe 2.41 / Calmar 3.18** with volatility-calibrated trailing stops is self-reported and unreviewed ([arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)).

| **Turtle pyramiding** | `N = ATR_20`; unit = 1% equity / N; add at +½N up to 4 units; all stops at 2N below the newest entry ([FXOpen](https://fxopen.com/blog/en/turtle-trading-system-rules-and-strategy)) | Sources disagree on 1% vs 2% ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/jbyeMkat/)); use 1h–1d bars to limit fees |
| **Vol targeting** | `w = min(1, σ_target/σ̂)`, σ̂ = EWMA or 20–60 day realized; Moreira-Muir `f^σ = (c*/σ̂²_{t−1})·f` ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)) | Harvey et al.: raises Sharpe for risk assets, US equity vol-of-vol 4.6%→1.8%, estimator choice barely matters ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). Out-of-sample gains are disputed in later literature (unverified) |
| **CPPI** | Cushion `C = V − F`; exposure `E = m·C`; the floor is breached if the asset drops > 1/m between rebalances; cash-lock at C = 0 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant_proportion_portfolio_insurance)) | Use m ≤ 3–5 given crypto gaps, with a ratcheting floor reset. No crypto study |
| **Drawdown de-risk** | TIPP floor `F = α·max V` (α = 0.8); or `scale = clip(1 − DD/DD_max, 0, 1)` | Standard; unverified source |
| **Carver scaling** | Position = vol scalar × forecast/10, where vol scalar = daily cash vol target / (price vol × block value); 10% position inertia ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24214)). FS = 10/mean\|raw\|, cap ±20 [unverified]. For crypto use √365 ≈ 19.1, not 16. Long-only: `max(0, forecast)` | Secondary summaries only |

With no leverage, vol targeting **can only de-risk**: the cap at 1 blocks levering up in calm regimes. That asymmetry is exactly what a long-only spot book wants. Carver's forecast scaling is valuable for a different reason. It converts the terminal's many signals into one comparable, capped position size, and its 10% inertia band is a ready-made turnover control under the 0.1% fee assumption.

## Execution and evaluation: fees dominate impact, and PSR should gate everything

Almgren-Chriss gives `x(t) = X·sinh(κ(T−t))/sinh(κT)` with `κ = √(λσ²/η)`, which collapses to TWAP at λ = 0 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almgren%E2%80%93Chriss_model)). The discrete form solves `κ = (1/τ)·arccosh(τ²κ̃²/2 + 1)` ([arXiv 1403.2229](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.2229)). VWAP scheduling (`q_k = Q·u_k/Σu_j`) and POV (`q_k = ρ·V_k`, ρ 5–10%) are standard but **unverified this session**. At retail size, with orders far below 0.1–1% of bar volume on liquid pairs, **fees and half-spread exceed impact**, so the optimal schedule is a single child order. The lever that matters is **post-only limit at the touch, a timeout, then a capped marketable limit**, with fees plus half-spread modeled as A-C's ε. Neither crypto intraday volume profiles nor maker fill probabilities by distance from the touch were found in any source, so calibrate both from the terminal's own fills. A-C and POV only matter for thin altcoins.

The PSR family is the most important metrics addition because crypto returns are fat-tailed and histories are short.

| Metric | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PSR** | `Φ((SR̂ − SR*)·√(n−1) / √(1 − γ₃SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)SR̂²))`, with SR̂ non-annualized and γ₄ raw kurtosis ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/17112/probabilistic-sharpe-ratio/)) | Report both PSR(0) and PSR vs buy-and-hold SR. PerformanceAnalytics ignores kurtosis by default ([rdrr](https://rdrr.io/pkg/PerformanceAnalytics/man/MinTrackRecord.html)) |
| **MinTRL** | `1 + [1 − γ₃SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)SR̂²]·(Φ⁻¹(1−α)/(SR̂ − SR*))²` | The source PDF was unreadable; the formula is consistent with Mertens' standard error ([SLCG](https://www.slcg.com/files/practice-notes/sharpe-ratio.pdf)) |
| **Ulcer / UPI** | `R_i = 100(C_i − max C)/max C`, `UI = √(ΣR_i²/N)`, `UPI = excess return/UI` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcer_index)) | Return convention differs between StockCharts and TradingView; sample at least weekly |
| **Omega** | `E[(R−L)⁺]/E[(L−R)⁺]` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_ratio)) | Sensitive to the threshold ([arXiv 2103.13806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.13806)); report L |
| **SQN** | `√N·mean(R)/sd(R)` on R-multiples ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/system-quality-number-sqn)) | N cap of 100 and the score bands are **unverified**; inflated by trade frequency |
| **K-ratio** | Slope of OLS on ln(equity) vs time / (SE·√n) | **Conflicting variants** (1996/2003/2013) ([Wealth-Lab](https://wl6.wealth-lab.com/Forum/Posts/-quot-K-Ratio-quot-Which-variation-40531)); state which one |
| Sortino, Calmar/MAR, Sterling, Burke, IR, tail ratio, gain-to-pain, STARR, Rachev [all unverified, standard definitions] | Sortino downside deviation `√((1/N)Σmin(0, r−T)²)`, divided by **total N**; Calmar = CAGR/\|MDD\| over 36 months, MAR the same over full history; Burke = (CAGR−r_f)/√ΣDD_j²; tail = \|Q₉₅\|/\|Q₀₅\|; gain-to-pain = Σr/\|Σr⁻\| monthly; STARR = mean/ES₅% | Resample to daily, A = 365; IR benchmark = BTC buy-and-hold |

MinTRL turns the gate's trade-count rule into a time requirement. Bailey & López de Prado's worked example (per-period SR 0.2, skew −1, kurtosis 5) needs about **85 observations** ([SLCG](https://www.slcg.com/files/practice-notes/sharpe-ratio.pdf)). That example is computed by the fetch tool from the formula, not quoted from the paper. Most crypto daily strategies will show lower per-period Sharpe and worse skew, so MinTRL will often exceed the out-of-sample window. Compute MinTRL before running a strategy's out-of-sample test, so the test is long enough to pass.

## Prioritized shortlist under the out-of-sample gate

The gate decides the order. **"≥100 OOS trades" kills single-asset, low-frequency rules unless trades are pooled across coins.** TOM produces 12 trades a year per coin, and a two-asset dual momentum may switch only a handful of times a year. **The fixed 0.2% round trip kills anything whose gross edge per trade is near 0.2%**: the 21–23 UTC trade grosses about 0.045% per trade (33% ÷ 730). **DSR p<0.05 and PBO<0.3 punish wide grids**, so each candidate should test at most about 4–8 pre-registered parameter sets, with the trial count logged.

| Priority | Item | Why now | Gate feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PSR, MinTRL, Sortino (total-N), Calmar/MAR, Ulcer/UPI, Omega(0), tail ratio, SQN (N≤100), K-ratio (stated variant) | Metrics only, no gate needed; MinTRL sizes every later OOS window | n/a |
| 2 | Purge-by-label-interval plus embargo in the tuning CV splitter, if missing | Stops leakage that inflates DSR and PBO for every ML candidate | n/a |
| 3 | Maker-first execution (post-only at the touch → timeout → capped marketable limit) | Real fees below the gate's 0.1% assumption add margin | n/a (execution) |
| 4 | Overlays on existing strategies: BTC regime gate, vol-target `w = min(1, σ*/σ̂)`, ratcheting Chandelier (22, 3) vs ATR vs time stop, in a 5-arm ablation | Strongest theory (Kaminski-Lo, Harvey et al.); reuses existing signals and trade counts | High: inherits existing trade frequency |
| 5 | CS momentum long-only top-k (7/14/21/28-day rank average, weekly, k = 3–5, rank buffer 2k, BTC gate) | Only missing family with peer-reviewed multi-coin crypto evidence | Good: ~3–10 trades a week across 20–30 coins gives 100 trades within months |
| 6 | Meta-labeling + bet sizing on the best existing primary signal | Long-only native; filters rather than adds exposure | Medium: needs ≥100 OOS meta-filtered trades; high overfitting risk, so count trials |
| 7 | Jump-model regime (λ by walk-forward net Sharpe) replacing raw HMM state | Equity OOS + costs evidence; directly cuts flicker turnover | Low as standalone (few switches); judge it as a gate on #4–#6 |
| 8 | Turn-of-month pooled over the top 10 coins, and dual momentum as an allocation layer | Tier B/C, low turnover, so costs are minor | TOM: ~120 trades a year pooled. Dual momentum: likely < 100, so evaluate as an overlay |
| 9 | 10-day-high Donchian preset and a 52-week-high CS rank variant | Cheap to add; reuses #5 infrastructure | Good if run cross-sectionally |
| 10 | Copula/Kalman cheap-leg filter and ETH/BTC rotation | Hypothesis only; long-short evidence will not transfer | Test after #5 |
| Defer | ORB, NR7/inside bar, CRSI, %b, CMF/OBV, CVD divergence, funding/basis (needs a futures feed), hourly seasonality standalone, Johansen/hedged pairs, A-C/VWAP/POV | Tier D, cost-infeasible, or needs short legs | Mostly fail the cost or trade-count test |

## Conclusion

Thousands of publicly documented strategies exist, but each of the gate's four criteria removes a different subset, and the four together leave only a few candidates. Peer-reviewed crypto alphas fail mainly because they live in short legs and illiquid tails. Practitioner crypto alphas fail mainly because they were never tested net of costs or out of sample. Definitional rules fail because nobody has tested them on crypto. Risk-side tools hold up: vol targeting, regime gating and wide volatility-scaled exits operate on the market-beta exposure that dominates long-only crypto P&L, rather than on fragile entry timing. The highest-expected-value roadmap is therefore to upgrade the measurement layer first (PSR, MinTRL, purged CV), then improve how existing signals hold and size positions, and only then add the one family with real evidence, CS momentum ranking. A strategy that later passes the gate will have done so against a corrected statistical standard.

The biggest open question is whether any of this holds on 2022–2026 Binance spot data. Almost every cited sample ends in or before 2021, or covers a bull-market window. The terminal can answer that question directly: its own walk-forward results on post-2022 data, net of 0.1% per side, will be stronger evidence than anything in this literature, and they should be recorded as a pre-registered result whether they pass or fail.
