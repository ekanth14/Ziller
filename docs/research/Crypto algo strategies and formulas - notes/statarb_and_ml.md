## Q1. Pairs-trading variants (distance, Kalman, copula, Johansen) and documented crypto results after costs

### Takeaway
All four variants are implementable on top of the existing cointegration/z-score stack; the only crypto results with explicit costs and rolling out-of-sample windows (Tadi & Witzany copula, Tadi & Kortchemski cointegration/Johansen) show positive net results, but both are short samples (about 1 to 2 years), on derivatives exchanges (futures/perps, so shorting was available), and single-exchange. The daily-frequency distance method on crypto is roughly zero (-0.07%/month); profits only appeared at intraday frequency, and Kalman hedge ratios raise turnover about 4 to 5x, which can flip the ranking vs rolling OLS once costs exceed a few bp.

### Cited Findings

**Distance method (Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst) on crypto**
- Fil & Kristoufek (IEEE Access 2020, vol. 8, pp. 172644-172651) tested distance and cointegration pairs on 26 Binance coins: the most common daily distance method returned -0.07% per month, rising to 11.61% per month at 5-minute frequency; results were "quite sensitive" to parameter settings, transaction costs and execution windows, and strategies underperformed classical benchmarks overall — [Search snippet of IEEE Access abstract via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c); [IEEE Xplore record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9200323). Evidence quality: abstract only; formation window, cost assumption and sample period of the 11.61% figure could NOT be verified (full text blocked).
- The same authors note intraday crypto prices show simple mean-reverting behaviour absent at daily frequency — [IEEE Access record (search snippet)](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c).
- An earlier Fil bachelor's thesis (Binance, 2018 data) found the distance method earned about 3% monthly under "baseline real-life conditions", but with cost assumptions borrowed from equity markets — [NUSL record (search snippet)](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/404258?ln=en). Do not mix with the IEEE figures.

**Kalman-filter dynamic hedge ratio**
- State-space model (Palomar, Portfolio Optimization book, Eq. 15.3): observation y1_t = [1, y2_t]·[mu_t, gamma_t]' + eps_t; state [mu_{t+1}, gamma_{t+1}]' = I·[mu_t, gamma_t]' + [eta1_t, eta2_t]' (random-walk states), eps ~ N(0, s2_eps), eta1 ~ N(0, s2_mu), eta2 ~ N(0, s2_gamma) — [Palomar 15.6](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html).
- Palomar's initialization heuristic from an LS training window of T_LS samples: s2_eps = Var[eps_LS]; mu_1 ~ N(mu_LS, Var[eps_LS]/T_LS); gamma_1 ~ N(gamma_LS, Var[eps_LS]/(T_LS·Var[y2])); s2_mu = alpha·Var[eps_LS]; s2_gamma = alpha·Var[eps_LS]/Var[y2]; alpha = 5e-5 chosen by trial and error (without costs) — [Palomar 15.6](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html).
- Traded spread (leverage-normalized, uses one-step-ahead prediction to avoid look-ahead): z_t = (y1_t - gamma_{t|t-1}·y2_t - mu_{t|t-1}) / (1 + gamma_{t|t-1}) — [Palomar 15.6](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html).
- Extensions: a "velocity" state (d gamma/dt) gave no appreciable improvement; a partial-cointegration model adds an AR(1) residual state with rho e.g. 0.9 — [Palomar 15.6](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html).
- Kalman vs rolling LS on EWA-EWC (equity ETFs, 2013-2022): no-cost cumulative return 0.78 (Kalman) vs 0.51 (rolling LS); Kalman turnover ~0.23/day vs 0.05/day (~4.5x); at 5 bp costs 0.56 vs 0.46; at 10 bp costs 0.33 vs 0.41 — ranking reverses. On a non-cointegrated pair (KO-PEP) with 2 bp costs Kalman lost (-0.03 vs +0.11) — [Palomar 15.6](https://portfoliooptimizationbook.com/book/15.6-kalman-pairs-trading.html). Evidence quality: equities, not crypto, but directly relevant to cost sensitivity.
- Crypto Kalman evidence is thin: a 2020 Medium study of Kalman-regression z-score on crypto USD pairs reports Sharpe ratios "rarely above 1" — [Medium/Analytics Vidhya](https://medium.com/analytics-vidhya/exploring-statistical-arbitrage-in-cryptocurrency-924ad6bf2a57); an open-source Binance 1h implementation uses intercept+slope state and dollar-neutral sizing (self-reported, unverified backtest) — [GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jing-Lavinia/Pairs-Trading/main/README.md); an EM-tuned noise-covariance Kalman (TU Delft thesis 2025) slightly beat unfiltered spreads but on only 3-4 trades — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:fecd3e7c-b12b-44ec-a628-914da816eeee).

**Copula method (crypto, with costs)**
- Tadi & Witzany (arXiv 2305.06961): 20 Binance USDT-M futures (BTCUSDT reference + 19 alts), hourly, 2021-01 to 2023-01. Spread S_t = BTCUSDT - beta·P_i; cointegration screened with Engle-Granger (ADF at 10%) and nonlinear KSS (10% crit -1.92); the two alts with the highest Kendall tau vs BTC form the pair; ~3 weeks formation / 1 week trading, 104 rolling cycles — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- Copula fitting: Gaussian, Student-t, Gumbel, Clayton, Frank, Joe, BB1, BB6, BB7, BB8, Tawn 1/2, plus 90/180/270 rotations; MLE parameters, AIC selection; most selected were Tawn Type 1 (~23-24%) and BB7 (15.4%) — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- Trading rule on conditional probabilities h(1|2) = dC(u1,u2)/du2 and h(2|1): open long S1/short S2 when h(1|2) < alpha1 AND h(2|1) > 1 - alpha1; reverse when h(1|2) > 1 - alpha1 AND h(2|1) < alpha1; close both when |h(1|2) - 0.5| < alpha2 AND |h(2|1) - 0.5| < alpha2; alpha1 in {0.10, 0.15, 0.20}, alpha2 = 0.10 — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- Costs: taker 0.04% assumed on all fills (maker 0.02%), initial capital 200k USDT — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- OOS results [OOS+COST]: EG alpha1=0.10: ann. return 37.1%, vol 38.2%, Sharpe 0.97, max DD -35.6%, 176 trades, costs = 11.7% of gross P&L. Looser thresholds degrade sharply (KSS alpha1=0.20: Sharpe 0.16, costs = 47.7% of gross P&L). Benchmarks: BTC buy-and-hold -17.0% ann. (Sharpe -0.23, DD -77.1%); equal-weight 20-coin B&H 14.4% ann. (Sharpe 0.15, DD -82.2%). No cointegration-only benchmark reported — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- Literature claim (Xie & Wu 2013, cited in the paper): distance and cointegration methods can be generalized as special cases of the copula method under certain conditions — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961).
- A 2026 AIMS QFE copula-crypto paper uses a baseline 0.08% round-trip cost and concludes transaction costs and design largely determine net performance; earlier documented pairs returns often shrink or vanish under realistic costs — [AIMS QFE 2026 (search snippet)](https://www.aimspress.com/aimspress-data/qfe/2026/2/PDF/QFE-10-02-016.pdf). A 2024 Financial Innovation paper combines copula and cointegration selection on crypto, but its post-cost numbers were not retrievable — [Financial Innovation s40854-024-00702-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-024-00702-7).

**Cointegration pairs vs Johansen baskets (crypto, with costs)**
- Tadi & Kortchemski (arXiv 2109.10662): BitMEX minute data, 27 Sep 2018 - 2 Oct 2019, 9 instruments (XBT perp, ETH quanto perp, futures on ETH/EOS/LTC/XRP/BCH/TRX/ADA vs XBT); 3-month formation, 1-week trading, 39 trading weeks — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662).
- Scenario 1 (pairs): OLS hedge, EG/ADF and KSS at 1%, choose shortest OU half-life (t1/2 = ln2/theta); z-score rolling window = half-life; enter at |z| = 2, exit inside |z| = 1. Scenario 2 (basket): Johansen test across all 9 coins, integer weights, prefer shortest half-life, portfolio p < 10% — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662).
- Costs/execution: maker -0.025% (rebate), taker 0.075%, funding +/-0.01% per 8h on perps; fills only if quoted size available, with >=1 period delay — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662).
- Reported OOS [OOS+COST, short sample]: pairs avg monthly return 17.3% (ADF) / 13.9% (KSS), Sharpe 6.96 / 6.57; Johansen basket realized P&L 1.44 XBT of which 0.81 XBT went to commission, Sharpe 7.94 (rf = 0). Scenario 3 (fixed pairs all year, Sharpe up to 20.38 for TRX-ADA) is hindsight-selected [IS] — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662).
- The same paper criticizes earlier crypto pairs studies: Driaunys et al. (2014) had no transaction costs, no risk-adjusted returns, no B&H benchmark; Broek & Sharif (2018) ignored costs and had selection bias; Leung & Nguyen (2019) found stop-loss exits reduced returns — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.10662).

### Inferences
- **Distance method formulas [UNVERIFIED-THIS-SESSION, Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst, RFS 2006]**: normalize each coin to a cumulative total-return index P~_i(t) = prod(1 + r_i) starting at 1 in the formation window; SSD_ij = sum_t (P~_i(t) - P~_j(t))^2; choose the N pairs (GGR: top 5/20) with smallest SSD; spread s_t = P~_i - P~_j (no hedge ratio, i.e. beta = 1); open when |s_t| > 2·sigma_s (sigma_s = formation-period std of the spread); close when spread crosses zero or at end of trading window; GGR used 12-month formation / 6-month trading and a one-day wait after signals to control bid-ask bounce. For crypto, the Fil & Kristoufek result implies scaling windows down drastically (formation in days, intraday bars).
- **Chan's Kalman parametrization [UNVERIFIED-THIS-SESSION, E. Chan, Algorithmic Trading (2013), Example 3.3 EWA-EWC]**: observation y_t = x_t·beta_t + e_t with x_t = [price_2(t), 1] and beta_t = [hedge, intercept]'; state beta_t = beta_{t-1} + w_t, w ~ N(0, Vw), Vw = delta/(1-delta)·I, e ~ N(0, Ve), with delta = 1e-4 and Ve = 1e-3. Recursion: R_t = P_{t-1} + Vw (P_0 = 0, beta_0 = 0); yhat_t = x_t·beta_{t-1}; Q_t = x_t R_t x_t' + Ve; e_t = y_t - yhat_t; K_t = R_t x_t'/Q_t; beta_t = beta_{t-1} + K_t e_t; P_t = R_t - K_t x_t R_t. Signals: long spread when e_t < -sqrt(Q_t), exit long when e_t > -sqrt(Q_t); short spread when e_t > +sqrt(Q_t), exit short when e_t < +sqrt(Q_t). Position: long 1 unit of y and short beta_hedge units of x. Larger delta = faster-adapting hedge ratio = more turnover. In Palomar's mapping, delta/(1-delta) plays the role of alpha·Var[eps]/Var[y2] (the state-to-observation noise ratio). Because e_t / sqrt(Q_t) is already a standardized innovation, it can replace the existing rolling z-score directly.
- Given Palomar's turnover result (4.5x) and crypto spot taker fees (typically 0.075-0.10% per side on major spot venues), Kalman hedging should be paired with a slower delta (e.g. 1e-5 to 1e-6) or a no-trade band; the 10 bp reversal in the equity example is a direct warning for spot crypto.
- Copula rule is attractive for a terminal because the entry/exit uses tail-aware conditional probabilities rather than a Gaussian z; but the only crypto net-of-cost evidence is one 2-year hourly study on futures. The terminal can reuse its Kendall-tau/cointegration screen and fit copulas with an off-the-shelf library (e.g. pyvinecopulib) per formation window.
- The positive crypto evidence (both Tadi papers) relied on futures/perps (shorting available, maker rebates in one case). Spot-only implementations cannot replicate the short leg; see Q2.
- Very high reported Sharpe values (6-8 on 39 weeks) should be treated with suspicion: short single-regime sample, single exchange, no Deflated Sharpe / PBO adjustment. The terminal's DSR/PBO tools should be applied before trusting any similar backtest.

### Gaps
- Full Fil & Kristoufek methodology (cost assumption, windows behind the 11.61%/month 5-minute figure) could not be retrieved (IEEE/DOAJ returned 403/empty).
- No study found that runs distance vs Kalman vs copula vs Johansen head-to-head on the same crypto universe with the same cost model.

## Q2. Long-only adaptations of stat-arb on spot (buy the cheap leg only, beta-aware without shorting)

### Takeaway
There is essentially no peer-reviewed evidence that crypto pairs trading survives as a long-only "buy the undervalued leg" strategy; the one direct comparison found (Nair 2021) reports that long-short versions outperformed long-only. Long-only spot implementations are best treated as a relative-value filter/rotation overlay on an otherwise directional position, not as market-neutral arbitrage.

### Cited Findings
- Nair (Investment Management and Financial Innovations, 2021) compared long-short pairs strategies with long-only portfolio strategies on cryptocurrencies over 2018-2019 and reports the long-short versions outperformed the conservative long-only ones — [Business Perspectives (search snippet)](https://businessperspectives.org/journals/investment-management-and-financial-innovations/issue-387/pairs-trading-in-cryptocurrency-market-a-long-short-story). Full text blocked (403); cost treatment unknown.
- arXiv 2405.15461 proposes a crypto pairs method that needs no external shorting and avoids holding crypto during the arbitrage period by trading a bucket of fiat currencies anchored to crypto — a fiat-vs-crypto construction, not crypto-vs-crypto — [arXiv 2405.15461](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v2).
- Practitioner explainers on ETH/BTC note that the spread view normally assumes a short leg (via CFDs/derivatives) and that costs and execution can erode the edge — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/amp/cryptocurrencies/news/what-is-bitcoin-pairs-trading-202412061012); [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/hk-pairs-trading-in-crypto-how-it-works-and-what-traders-should-note-before-using-it).
- In the copula study, BTC buy-and-hold had max DD -77.1% and the equal-weight alt basket -82.2% over 2021-2023 — [arXiv 2305.06961](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06961) — which quantifies the market-beta exposure a long-only "cheap leg" position would carry versus the hedged pair's -35.6% DD.

### Inferences
Implementable long-only variants (none have documented crypto results found in this session; classify as hypotheses to test with the terminal's CPCV/PBO/DSR):
1. **Cheap-leg-only entry (half-pair)**: compute the existing spread z (or Kalman e/sqrt(Q), or copula h(i|j)) of coin i vs benchmark j (BTC, ETH, or a sector basket). Buy coin i when z_i < -z_entry (e.g. -2) or h(i|j) < alpha1; exit when z_i > -z_exit (e.g. -0.5/0) or at a half-life-based time stop (e.g. 2-3x OU half-life). Return decomposes as beta_i·r_benchmark + (convergence of the spread); only the second term is the stat-arb edge, so it is only worth trading when the benchmark trend is not negative (combine with the HMM regime filter: allow entries only in non-bear states).
2. **Rotation between legs (long-only "switch")**: hold whichever leg is cheap. With capital in coin j, when z(i vs j) < -z_entry, swap j -> i; swap back when z > +z_entry. This captures both sides of the spread's oscillation without shorting, at the cost of always being 100% exposed to the pair's common factor. On spot, each swap costs two taker fees (sell j, buy i via a USDT leg) unless a direct i/j market exists (e.g. ETH/BTC spot), which halves the cost — check pair availability.
3. **Relative-value filter on a directional signal**: when a directional model (momentum, ML meta-label) fires buys on several coins, rank candidates by spread z vs benchmark and only take the most undervalued (most negative z / lowest copula conditional probability). This is the lowest-risk use: it changes selection, not exposure.
4. **Beta-adjusted cash hedge**: size the long leg as w_i and hold (1 - w_i·beta_i)-ish in stablecoin so that portfolio beta to BTC is controlled; this reduces but cannot eliminate market exposure (beta can only be lowered to 0 by holding cash, never made negative).
- Expected effect: the long-only variants lose the hedge that made the published Sharpe ratios possible; drawdowns will look more like buy-and-hold than like the hedged pair. Treat the Q1 results as an upper bound, not a baseline, for spot.

### Gaps
- No paper found with documented after-cost results for half-pair / cheap-leg-only crypto stat-arb on spot.
- Nair (2021) details (pair selection, costs, magnitude of the long-short vs long-only gap) unavailable (403).
- No source found on rotation ("switch") strategies on ETH/BTC spot with costs.

## Q3. ML setups: GBM/RF on triple-barrier labels, meta-labeling, crypto feature sets, uniqueness weights, fractional differentiation, purged k-fold; OOS after costs and failure modes

### Takeaway
The López de Prado pipeline (event sampling -> triple-barrier/meta-labels -> uniqueness weights -> FFD features -> purged/CPCV -> bet sizing) is fully specified and implementable, but crypto papers that use it report net-of-cost results mostly in bull-market OOS windows, and none found combine GBM + meta-labeling + purged CV + costs on crypto. The single most-repeated caveat is overfitting through barrier/threshold tuning and bullish test periods.

### Cited Findings

**Fractional differentiation**
- Weights: omega_k = (-1)^k · prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (d - i)/k!, i.e. omega = {1, -d, d(d-1)/2!, -d(d-1)(d-2)/3!, ...}; for integer d weights are 0 for k > d — [mlfinpy docs](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html). (Equivalent recursion: omega_0 = 1, omega_k = -omega_{k-1}·(d - k + 1)/k.)
- Expanding window X~_t = sum_k omega_k X_{t-k} causes "a severe negative drift" without weight-loss control; weight-loss lambda_l = sum_{j=T-l}^{T}|omega_j| / sum_{i=0}^{T-l}|omega_i|; choose l* such that lambda_{l*} <= tau and lambda_{l*+1} > tau — [mlfinpy docs](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html).
- Fixed-width window (FFD): omega~_k = omega_k for k <= l*, 0 otherwise; X~_t = sum_{k=0}^{l*} omega~_k X_{t-k}. mlfinpy `frac_diff_ffd` default weight cutoff thresh = 1e-5; open-quant default 1e-4 — [mlfinpy docs](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html); [open-quant fracdiff (search snippet)](https://open-quant.github.io/openquant/modules/fracdiff).
- d selection: apply FFD over a grid of d (e.g. 0 to 1 in 0.05 steps) to log prices, compute ADF on the (daily-downsampled) result, take the minimum d whose ADF stat is below the 95% critical value; also track corr(original, differenced) to see memory retained — [mlfinpy docs](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html). Example: in a Chinese futures study the 95% ADF critical value was -2.8618 and the crossing occurred near d = 0.3 — [arXiv 2303.04581 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.04581).
- Crypto use: Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski re-select the fractional order by ADF in each walk-forward segment — [arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2).

**Triple-barrier ML on crypto (documented setups)**
- Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski (arXiv 2411.12753): BTC, ETH, LTC; Binance 1-min OHLC aggregated to 10/20/30-min trading bars; Jan 2016 - Apr 2022, OOS Jan 2020 - Apr 2022; features = six fractionally differenced macro/commodity series (ICSA jobless claims, WTI, Henry Hub gas, corn, gold, copper/aluminium); supervised autoencoder MLP with noise augmentation (0.1 noise ratio); walk-forward validation; costs 0.05% per open/close (two charges on a flip) — [arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2).
- Reported OOS [OOS+COST, but bullish window, no slippage]: BTC 30-min IR 2.03, max DD 14.02% (B&H total 406%, DD 53.3%); ETH 20-min IR 2.64, total 1658.9% vs B&H 1929.0%; LTC 30-min total 382.5% vs B&H 118.9%; equal-weight portfolio 20-min 619.8% vs B&H 595.2% — [arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2).
- Their own caveats: test set "remained a highly bullish time", strategy returns highly correlated across assets, slippage ignored, SL/TP assumed filled perfectly, timed-exit penalty delta = 20 set arbitrarily, barrier width/holding length not fully specified — [arXiv 2411.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2). The supervised-autoencoder paper also found excessive noise or large bottlenecks impair performance — [arXiv 2404.01866](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01866v2).
- A Financial Innovation (2025) study on BTC/ETH reports CUSUM-filtered bars + triple-barrier labels beat time bars and next-bar prediction and stayed "consistently positive" after transaction costs — [Financial Innovation s40854-025-00866-w (search snippet)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-025-00866-w). Model class, cost level and numbers not retrievable (login redirect).
- A Bitcoin triple-barrier paper warns that experimenting with barrier distances can lead to overfitting, "very dangerous in financial markets" — [WNE Warsaw WP (search snippet)](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/download_file/3896/0).
- A March 2026 preprint comparing RF, XGBoost and LightGBM on Bitcoin with expanding-window (not purged) validation reports a gross 2025 OOS return of +35.97% (185 trades, SL 1%, TP 2%, prob threshold 0.7) with Sharpe 0.14 before costs — [Preprints.org (search snippet)](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/384a120c45428509aa846cf34b2c8121/download_pub). [OOS, NO COST, not peer-reviewed] — a gross Sharpe of 0.14 with 185 trades would very likely turn negative after spot fees.

**Meta-labeling**
- Joubert, "Meta-Labeling: Theory and Framework", Journal of Financial Data Science 4(3), Summer 2022, pp. 31-44; follow-ups: Meyer, Barziy & Joubert, "Meta-Labeling: Calibration and Position Sizing", JFDS 5(2), 2023, pp. 23-40; Thumm, Barucca & Joubert on ensemble meta-labeling report ensembles help most when data has multiple regimes and nonlinear structure — [Hudson & Thames meta-labeling repo mirror / search snippets](https://github.onedrive11.workers.dev/hudson-and-thames/meta-labeling); [Hudson & Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/?p=3454). Full texts not retrieved.
- Practitioner discussion argues meta-labeling is "not a silver bullet" — it cannot create edge if the primary model has none — [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14706/why-meta-labeling-is-not-a-silver-bullet/).
- No crypto paper found that reports meta-labeling + triple-barrier results with costs — [search result summary, see Financial Innovation and arXiv items above].

### Inferences
Implementation recipe [UNVERIFIED-THIS-SESSION, López de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning (2018), chapters 2-7 and 10]:
- **Event sampling (ch.2)**: symmetric CUSUM filter S+_t = max(0, S+_{t-1} + y_t - E_{t-1}[y_t]), S-_t = min(0, S-_{t-1} + y_t - E_{t-1}[y_t]); emit an event and reset when S+_t > h or S-_t < -h; set h ~ k·daily vol (e.g. 1-2x EWM std of returns). Train only on events, not every bar.
- **Meta-labeling (ch.3)**: primary model (e.g. existing momentum/z-score/copula rule) sets side s in {-1, +1} (on spot, long-only: s = +1 only). Apply triple-barrier with that side; meta-label = 1 if the trade hit the profit-take barrier (or ended with positive return at the vertical barrier), else 0. Secondary classifier (RF/GBM) predicts P(label = 1 | features). Tune the primary for high recall; the meta-model raises precision/F1. This is natively long-only compatible: the secondary model just filters which long signals to take.
- **Bet sizing from probability (ch.10)**: z = (p - 1/2)/sqrt(p(1-p)); size m = 2·Phi(z) - 1 (binary case), then average across concurrently active bets and discretize (step e.g. 0.05-0.1) to cut turnover. On spot long-only, clip m to [0, 1].
- **Sample uniqueness (ch.4)**: indicator 1_{t,i} = 1 if label i's interval [t_i0, t_i1] spans bar t; concurrency c_t = sum_i 1_{t,i}; uniqueness u_{t,i} = 1_{t,i}/c_t; average uniqueness ubar_i = mean of u_{t,i} over label i's life. Return-attribution weight w~_i = |sum_{t=t_i0}^{t_i1} r_t / c_t|, normalized to sum to number of samples; optional linear time decay. Sequential bootstrap draws samples with probability proportional to their average uniqueness given already-drawn samples. In sklearn: RandomForest with max_samples ~ mean(ubar_i) (or BaggingClassifier with max_samples = average uniqueness), class_weight='balanced_subsample', max_features low (1 to sqrt) to reduce overfitting; GBM: pass w~_i as sample_weight.
- **Purged k-fold (ch.7)**: for each test fold, drop from training any observation whose label interval [t_i0, t_i1] overlaps the test fold's time span (purge), and additionally drop training observations within an embargo after the test fold, h ~ 0.01·T bars. The terminal already has CPCV/PBO; the pieces it may lack are the purge-by-label-interval and the embargo inside the CV splitter used for hyperparameter tuning.
- **FFD features**: apply to log price, log volume, and spreads with the minimum-d per coin; re-estimate d inside each walk-forward fold to avoid leakage (as Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski did). AFML reports that liquid futures typically need well below d = 1 (the book's E-mini example is around d ~ 0.35 with very high correlation to the original) — recall only, not verified.
- **Crypto feature sets observed**: macro/commodity series fractionally differenced (Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski); price/volume bar features on CUSUM-sampled bars (Financial Innovation 2025). The terminal's Alpha158 factors plus FFD price, rolling vol, OU half-life/z-score, Hurst and HMM state probabilities form a reasonable feature set; add cross-asset features (BTC return/vol, funding if available as an exogenous signal).
- **Common failure modes** (synthesized from cited caveats): (1) bullish OOS windows flatter long-biased ML (Ślepaczuk & Bieganowski); (2) barrier/threshold over-tuning (WNE WP); (3) costs and slippage ignored or SL/TP assumed perfectly filled; (4) gross Sharpe near zero that cannot survive fees (Preprints 2026); (5) correlated strategy returns across coins = fewer independent bets than it looks; (6) non-purged validation leaking overlapping labels; (7) meta-labeling cannot rescue a primary model with no edge (QuantConnect). Use DSR with the true number of trials and PBO on every tuned grid.

## Q4. Regime-switching strategies (Markov switching) with published trading rules

### Takeaway
Published crypto Markov/HMM work is mostly forecasting, not trading; the one traded crypto Markov-switching write-up found lost money out of sample, and the best-documented regime-trading evidence (equities, 1990-2023, with costs and delays) found statistical jump models beat both HMM-guided allocation and buy-and-hold. For a spot terminal the defensible rule is a long/flat (risk-on/cash) switch, which is long-only compatible.

### Cited Findings
- Shu, Yu & Mulvey (arXiv 2402.05272, v3 Sep 2024): statistical jump models (JM) improve regime persistence via a jump penalty applied at each state transition; features are risk/return measures computed only from the return series; the jump penalty is chosen by time-series cross-validation that directly optimizes strategy performance; tested OOS on US, German and Japanese equity indices 1990-2023 including transaction costs and trading delays; JM-guided strategy reduced volatility and max drawdown and improved Sharpe versus both an HMM-guided strategy and buy-and-hold — [arXiv 2402.05272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05272). Abstract gives no numeric figures.
- Multi-state HMMs on BTC, ETH and XRP were evaluated by out-of-sample forecasting; a four-state specification gave the best OOS forecasting performance and a bear regime with negative returns and high volatility was identified — forecasting accuracy, not a traded strategy — [arXiv 2011.03741 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03741.pdf).
- A practitioner Markov-switched strategy on BTC/ETH 4-hour bars lost money out of sample — [Signal Independence Audit, MSS post](https://hallengray.github.io/signal-independence-audit/MSS_POST.html). Informal, not peer-reviewed.
- A regime-switching RL crypto portfolio paper defines three regimes from volatility and return quantiles — [arXiv 2310.04536 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.04536v1).
- Markov regime switching has also been used for Bitcoin allocation within international portfolios (asset allocation, not timing) — [arXiv 2205.00335](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00335).

### Inferences
- **Jump model objective [UNVERIFIED-THIS-SESSION; Nystrup et al. / Shu-Yu-Mulvey formulation]**: given standardized feature vectors x_t and K centroids theta_k, choose state sequence s_t to minimize sum_t ||x_t - theta_{s_t}||^2 + lambda·sum_t 1{s_t != s_{t-1}}; alternate between dynamic-programming (Viterbi-like) state assignment and centroid updates (k-means-like). Lambda trades persistence vs responsiveness and should be selected by walk-forward CV on strategy Sharpe net of costs, as in the paper. Typical features: EWM downside deviation and EWM mean return / Sortino over several half-lives (e.g. 5, 10, 21 days) — recall, verify in the paper's full text.
- **Implementable spot rule (long-only)**: fit the existing HMM (or a 2-state JM) on rolling/expanding windows; at each bar compute the filtered (not smoothed) bull-state probability; hold the asset when P(bull) > 0.5 (or require persistence, e.g. predicted state unchanged for n bars), else hold stablecoin; trade with a one-bar delay and charge taker fees per switch. Smoothed probabilities use future data and must not drive signals.
- **Regime as a gate for stat-arb**: allow mean-reversion (pairs/cheap-leg) entries only in low-volatility/non-trending states and allow momentum/ML-long entries only in bull states; this is where regime models most plausibly add value for a spot terminal, and it is directly testable with CPCV/PBO.
- The jump-penalty idea is the main upgrade over a plain HMM: HMM state flicker generates turnover that costs eat, which is consistent with both the JM paper's finding and the failed MSS write-up.

- Numeric results, cost level, delay and jump-penalty value in Shu, Yu & Mulvey were not available from the abstract; full text needed.
